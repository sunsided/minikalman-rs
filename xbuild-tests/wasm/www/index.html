Outliers: trust, reject or down-weight

correct, set_gate, correct_robust with RobustWeight::StudentT

A sensor reads a moving position with Gaussian noise, and some readings are gross outliers. Three filters with the same model see the same readings. The plain one believes every reading. The gated one rejects readings whose normalized innovation squared (NIS) exceeds the gate. The robust one models the noise as Student-t and down-weights a reading the further it is from the prediction.

All three assume constant velocity with random acceleration. The true path is a smooth swing that this model can follow, plus, if you turn up target jump, a sudden step at 15 s that no motion model predicts.

What you're seeing: with the default knobs, both the gate and the Student-t weight cut the error of the plain filter to about a third, and the gate rejects almost exactly the injected outliers. Now set target jump to 3 m. The plain filter follows the step (and keeps flinching at every outlier). The robust filter distrusts the first readings after the step, its uncertainty grows, and within about a second it follows too. The gated filter sees a NIS far above its gate for every reading after the step, rejects them all and coasts on its model, drifting away: a gate lockout, visible in the NIS trace below. Turn on re-acquire: after that many rejections in a row it inflates its covariance 100-fold, the next reading passes, and it snaps back. Set it too low with many outliers and bursts of outliers trigger it too.

Read the experiment in outliers.rs, and the docs of correct_robust and set_gate.

Numerics: when the variance drops below one bit

CovarianceUpdate::Standard vs Joseph, in f32, I16F16 and I32F32

Ten precise position sensors at 18° steps update a 2D tracker one after another. That drives the position variance in P towards about 10⁻⁵, below the smallest step of Q16.16 fixed point (2⁻¹⁶ ≈ 1.5·10⁻⁵). The same run is computed six times: three number types, each with the short update P − K·H·P (solid) and the Joseph form (dashed).

What you're seeing: f32 and I32F32 stay positive definite with either form; the Joseph form keeps P exactly symmetric, the short form leaves rounding asymmetry. In I16F16 the short form loses definiteness in a few steps but keeps tracking, and the Joseph form does not rescue it: it diverges. The cause is resolution, not the form. Raise R to 10⁻² and Q16.16 is fine again; otherwise use a type with more fractional bits.

Read the experiment in numerics.rs, the crate's numerical robustness bench, and the docs of CovarianceUpdate.

Smoothing: hindsight through a gap

smoother::RtsSmoother, smoother::FixedLagSmoother

A sensor goes quiet for a few seconds while the target keeps turning. The filter knows only the past, so in the gap it coasts in a straight line and its uncertainty grows. The fixed-lag smoother refines each step with the next 1.5 s of readings, online. The RTS smoother runs backwards over the recorded run, so every step also sees the readings after the gap.

What you're seeing: in a 3 s gap the filter misses the target by several metres; the RTS smoother bends its estimate through the gap to meet the readings on the other side, with a fraction of the error and a much tighter ±2σ band. Outside the gap, smoothing still roughly halves the error. A smoother needs the future, so use it for logs and calibration runs, or accept the fixed lag.

Read the experiment in smoothing.rs, and the docs of the smoother module.

Parameter estimation: two estimators or one

rls::RecursiveLeastSquares + regular::RegularKalman vs extended::ExtendedKalman

A motor's speed follows xₖ₊₁ = a·xₖ + b·uₖ plus noise, and at step 400 the load changes: (a, b) jumps from (0.9, 0.5) to (0.7, 1.0). The dual estimator identifies (a, b) with recursive least squares and feeds them to a one-state Kalman filter. The joint estimator appends a and b to the state and runs one extended Kalman filter on [x, a, b], whose covariance links every speed residual to the parameters.

What you're seeing: after the load change the joint filter re-identifies the plant in 25 steps, the dual one in 82, and with this seed the joint one also settles closer. The dual estimator has the lower speed error while the model holds. Both have a memory knob: set q to 10⁻⁸ and the joint filter stops learning; set μ to 1 and the dual one does.

Read the experiment in parameters.rs, the augmented_state and rls_adaptive examples, and the docs of rls.
