Tracking a moving target

Radars measure range and bearing to a target circling past them. Range noise spreads a measurement along the line of sight; bearing noise spreads it sideways, more so the further away the target is. Together they make a crescent in x/y. Both filters approximate it with a Gaussian ellipse: the Extended filter by linearizing at its estimate, the Unscented filter by pushing sigma points through the measurement function. Both run on the same measurements.

Behind a radar, the bearing jumps from +180° to −180°. With wrap bearing residuals on, both filters use angles::wrap_pi to see that as the small step it is. Turn it off and restart: when the target passes behind the radar, both filters see a jump of almost a full turn and are thrown off. With very little bearing noise the Unscented filter can coast through instead, because its gain collapses rather than applying the jump. Turn on the second radar to see how two crescents intersect into a much tighter estimate.

— true path ∙ radar 1 measurement ∙ radar 2 measurement — EKF estimate, 2σ ellipse — UKF estimate, 2σ ellipse
